How Many Hiragana Are There? 46 Basic Kana and 104 Total

There are 46 basic hiragana. Add 20 voiced kana and 5 semi-voiced kana for 71. Add 33 contracted sounds for 104. But you only need to learn 46 basic shapes. The others add marks to those shapes or combine two kana. Here is how each group works, and why the kana chart does not have 50 basic characters.

When people ask how many hiragana there are, they usually mean 46. Those are the basic characters shown in textbooks, handwriting practice books, and kana charts.

2. 46 basic hiragana: Why not 50?

The kana chart has 10 rows and 5 columns, making 50 spaces. Only 45 spaces contain characters used today. Add ん, which sits outside the chart, and you get 46.

aiueo
あ rowあいうえお
か rowかきくけこ
さ rowさしすせそ
た rowたちつてと
な rowなにぬねの
は rowはひふへほ
ま rowまみむめも
や rowやゆよ
ら rowらりるれろ
わ rowわを
んん

There are 5 empty spaces. The i and e spaces in the や row and the u space in the わ row have no separate characters in modern Japanese. The i and e spaces in the わ row once held ゐ and ゑ, but Modern Kana Usage no longer uses them. Its historical spelling table shows where they appeared: older spellings of 居る used ゐ, while older spellings of 絵 and 声 used ゑ. Today they are written with い and え.

Modern Kana Usage lists ん separately as a nasal sound. It belongs to no row, so many kana charts put it at the end.

を is another special case. It sounds the same as お. Under Modern Kana Usage, it is written only as a particle, as in 本を読む. When you’re starting out, remember it as the character used for that particle.

3. 25 voiced and semi-voiced kana

Add two marks (゛) at the upper right to make a voiced kana. Add a small circle (゜) to make a semi-voiced kana. The basic shape stays the same.

aiueo
が rowがぎぐげご
ざ rowざじずぜぞ
だ rowだぢづでど
ば rowばびぶべぼ
ぱ rowぱぴぷぺぽ

Only the か, さ, た, and は rows take the two marks. Only the は row takes the small circle.

ぢ and づ sound the same as じ and ず. Usually, you write じ and ず. Modern Kana Usage specifies two situations where you write ぢ and づ instead.

  • When a sound repeats and the second occurrence becomes voiced, as in ちぢむ（縮む） and つづく（続く）.
  • When words join and the second word originally starts with ち or つ, as in はなぢ（鼻血） and みかづき（三日月）.

You do not need to memorize 25 new shapes. Just learn which rows take the two marks or the circle.

4. 33 contracted sounds

A contracted sound pairs a kana from the i column with a small ゃ, ゅ, or ょ. You read the pair as one sound. For example, き＋ゃ is kya, and し＋ょ is sho.

ししゃしゅしょ
ちちゃちゅちょ
ににゃにゅにょ

みみゃみゅみょ
りりゃりゅりょ
ぎぎゃぎゅぎょ
じじゃじゅじょ
びびゃびゅびょ
ぴぴゃぴゅぴょ

That is 11 rows with 3 sounds each, or 33. The contracted-sound table in Modern Kana Usage also lists ぢゃ, ぢゅ, and ぢょ. Like ぢ in the previous section, they appear only in certain words, so standard kana charts leave them out. Include them and the count is 36.

Modern Kana Usage says to write ゃ ゅ ょ small whenever possible. If you write a full-size や instead, きや is read as two sounds, ki-ya.

5. Small っ and long vowels are not new characters

The small っ marks a brief pause, as in がっこう（学校） and はしって（走って）. It is a small version of つ, so it is not usually counted as a separate character.

Long vowels do not add new characters either. In hiragana, you add a vowel kana, as in おかあさん, くうき（空気）, and おとうさん. Katakana uses the long-vowel mark “ー” instead.

6. Katakana has the same counts

Each hiragana has a matching katakana. Katakana has 46 basic characters, with the same counts for voiced kana, semi-voiced kana, and contracted sounds. The difference is their shape. Hiragana developed from cursive forms of kanji, while most katakana came from parts of kanji. For the kanji behind each hiragana, see my hiragana origins chart.

7. What to learn first

Based on these groups, here is the order I recommend.

  1. Get comfortable writing the 46 basic hiragana first. All the later forms build on these shapes.
  2. Then learn the 25 voiced and semi-voiced kana. Remember that か さ た は can take the two marks, and は can take the circle.
  3. Learn the 33 contracted sounds last. Write the small kana small, and read each pair as one beat.

Among the 46 basic characters, lookalikes such as ぬ and め, る and ろ, and は and ほ often take the most time. I recommend writing them from memory instead of just looking at a chart. I explain how in Can’t Remember the Kana? Write Them From Memory. For stroke counts and easily confused stroke orders, see How to Write Hiragana Stroke Order.

9. Frequently asked questions

How many hiragana are there?

There are 46 basic hiragana, including ん. Add 20 voiced kana and 5 semi-voiced kana for 71. Add 33 contracted sounds for 104.

Why does the kana chart have only 46 characters if it has 50 spaces?

The chart has 10 rows and 5 columns, but modern Japanese uses characters in only 45 of those spaces. The i and e spaces in the や row and the u space in the わ row have no characters. ゐ and ゑ in the わ row are no longer used. Add ん from outside the chart for a total of 46.

Will I still see ゐ and ゑ?

Modern Kana Usage no longer uses these characters. They appear in historical spellings. Today they are written as い and え.

How many katakana are there?

There are also 46 basic katakana. The counts for voiced kana, semi-voiced kana, and contracted sounds match hiragana. Each hiragana has a corresponding katakana.

Should I learn hiragana or katakana first?

I recommend hiragana first. Japanese uses it for particles and the endings of verbs and adjectives. Katakana is used mainly for loanwords. Both sets have the same counts and pronunciations, so after hiragana, you only need to learn the new shapes.
